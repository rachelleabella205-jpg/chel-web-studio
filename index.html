Sound Familiar?

The problem usually isn’t your products.

Many Filipino small business owners work hard every day: posting consistently, replying to messages, and joining online selling communities. But sometimes the challenge is how customers discover, browse, and order.

  • Hours on Messenger. Replying to inquiries, sending product photos and price lists, confirming orders, and checking payments one by one.
  • Waiting for walk-ins. Owning a physical store but hoping more people will discover your products.
  • Too many chats at once. Keeping track of messages, orders, and payments when several customers inquire at the same time.
My Story

Why I started CHEL Web Studio

Chelle Abella
Founder, CHEL Web Studio

Before this journey, I started a digital printables and printing business. Through it, I became part of Facebook communities and met other sellers whose creativity, hard work, and determination I truly admired.

But I noticed a common challenge: many were managing almost everything manually through Facebook. Customers asked for prices, requested details, confirmed orders, and arranged payments through private messages. For some, every sale meant another long conversation.

Traditional businesses and physical stores face their own challenges too, often depending on walk-in customers and social media visibility. That’s when I started asking:

What if customers could browse products, check prices, and place orders more conveniently?

What if business owners could present their products professionally instead of relying only on posts and Messenger?

What if customers could discover a business even when they weren’t near the store?

Those questions led me to learn e-commerce website development using Shopify and AI-assisted tools, and that’s how CHEL Web Studio began to take shape.

My goal is simple: to help traditional Filipino businesses take their next step online, because even small, home-based, and traditional businesses deserve the chance to grow.

Why a Website

Five ways a website can help your business.

A Facebook page is a great starting point. A website complements it by giving customers another way to discover and interact with your business.

01

A dedicated online home

Organize your offerings into categories so customers can browse products, descriptions, and prices in one place, instead of scrolling through months of posts.

02

More convenient ordering

Depending on setup, customers can add to cart, submit orders, choose payment methods, and receive confirmations, reducing repetitive Messenger conversations.

03

Reach beyond your community

A physical store serves people who can visit. A website can introduce your products to other towns and cities, with the right delivery arrangements and marketing.

04

A more professional presence

Showcase your brand, products, story, policies, and contact details in one place so potential customers can decide if you’re the right fit.

05

A foundation for growth

Your online store can become part of a broader strategy that includes social media, search visibility, and customer retention.

Facebook + Website

Your website doesn’t have to replace Facebook. The two can work together.

Free Business Tip

5 things to prepare before building your website.

You don’t need hundreds of products or a complicated site to begin. A simple, well-organized online store can be a practical first step.

  1. Organize your product listNames, prices, descriptions, sizes or variations, and stock information.
  2. Prepare clear product photosBright, accurate photos, kept organized for easy uploading.
  3. Decide how customers will orderPay online, pay upon delivery where available, or submit an order for confirmation.
  4. Plan delivery or pickupService areas, delivery fees, preparation times, and store pickup options.
  5. Make your business info easy to findBusiness name, logo, contact details, operating hours, social links, and basic policies.
A Little Reminder

Your business may be local, but your opportunities don’t have to stop at your doorstep. 🇵🇭

You don’t need to be a big company to build your online presence. You don’t need to abandon your physical store, and you don’t need to stop using Facebook.

Of course, a website alone doesn’t guarantee more sales. It still takes good products, customer service, effective marketing, and a convenient buying experience. But when these work together, your business can be better prepared to serve customers and pursue new opportunities.
